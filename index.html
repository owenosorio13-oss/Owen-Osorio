Why finance

My interest in finance started with my best friend from high school. He graduated a year ahead of me and is now studying finance at Wharton. Over the last few years, he's shared what he's been learning about markets with me, and that inspired me to pursue it myself. Numbers and economics have always come naturally to me, so finance felt like a field where my strengths would fit.

In high school, I became president of the Investment Club, and it turned into my passion project. I wanted to share my understanding and love of investing with as many people as I could. I promoted the club until it became the largest at my school, with over 250 members. I built a website to organize our meeting notes and resources, and I spent hours each week preparing sessions so they'd be engaging and students would actually learn from them. The most rewarding moments were when a student came up after a session with a question. It meant they were really engaging, and that I was really teaching them something.

I believe understanding investing early matters for everyone, no matter their situation. Since then, my interest has grown from investing into finance as a whole, especially venture capital. I'm building a startup of my own, so I've become fascinated by how firms decide which young companies to fund and what they see in them before anyone else does. That's part of why I'm competing in the University Growth Fund's VC Case Competition this fall.

Long term, I want a career that lets me build a stable future for my family while doing work that keeps me learning. Finance is the right path for both.

What I'm building

Pipeline is an iOS app I'm building on my own. It turns a student's career goal into a dated plan of milestones, plus three small actions to take each day, and it reroutes the plan when the goal changes. I built it because I was in the same position I think many of my users are in: motivated and willing to work hard, but unsure where to start or how to get ahead. I wanted to build the tool I wished I'd had.

I built it in SwiftUI on Supabase, with the Claude API behind a secure server, and wrote around 570 automated tests to make sure it holds up. It's now feature-complete and preparing for App Store release. Building it alone taught me how hard it is to finish something once the exciting part is over, and it made me start thinking like a founder: who the product is really for, how to reach them, and whether it could stand on its own as a business. That's a big part of why I'm curious about how investors evaluate companies like mine.
